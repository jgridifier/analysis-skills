The short version

  • What it is. Empirical Bayes for the normal-means problem: many noisy observations \(x_i=\theta_i+\varepsilon_i\), a prior \(G\) estimated from the whole set, and a posterior mean plus a local false sign rate for each \(i\). Fifteen prior families in one R package, from a point-normal spike-and-slab to a nonparametric scale mixture (ash).
  • Status: promoted (Jared, 2026-11-13, 11:51 PM ET). Teaching page: 2026-11-13 scout, method 1. Approval: APPROVAL.md.
  • Novelty: niche. Standard in statistical genetics; less used as a general tool for noisy program-level series.
  • Evidence limit: the formal three-application pipeline on this repo's real panels has not been run. The numbers below are from a SYNTHETIC mock-budget scout, plus a --synthetic self-test.

02

The math, plainly

Each unit gives an observation \(x_i \sim N(\theta_i, s_i^2)\) and a known (or carefully estimated) standard error \(s_i\). The unknown means \(\theta_i\) are drawn from a prior \(G\) that is itself estimated from \(\{(x_i,s_i)\}\). The package returns the posterior mean \(\hat\theta_i = E(\theta_i \mid x_i, s_i, \hat G)\) and the local false sign rate, the posterior probability that a reported sign is wrong. A small lfsr is a call that the move is real and of that sign. The default starting family here is ash, a mixture of zero-mean normals at many scales; point-normal (a spike at the mode plus one normal slab) is the usual companion.

03

Why it is unusual, and fast

  • One interface covers many prior families, so a flexible mixture and a simple baseline can be compared without switching packages.
  • lfsr is a built-in, signed alternative to a BH-adjusted z-test: on the Corning mock it called 91 real year-on-year moves with 0 wrong signs, against 14 for BH.
  • The fits themselves are cheap (tenths of a second for a thousand observations on the scout). The work is in getting honest \(s_i\).
