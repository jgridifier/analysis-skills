Weekly rate and stress regimes: 35 breaks, and a null that finds just as many

On weekly changes of six series the break dates reproduce exactly, but a stationary VAR(1) null produces 20–37 breaks too. Prewhitening leaves 12 stable breaks.

The short version

  • On weekly changes of six rate and stress series (1994–2026), changeforest finds 35 breaks, and 28 of them recur in at least 8 of 10 seeds. The mean-change test finds 0. Every number reproduced exactly.
  • But a break-free VAR(1) with the same persistence (spectral radius 0.957) gets 20–37 breaks. As they stand, the 35 can't be told apart from autocorrelation.
  • After prewhitening (rerunning on VAR(1) residuals) there are 22 breaks, 12 stable, and the null drops to 0–2. Those 12 are the defensible regime dates.
01

Question

When did the joint behaviour of rates, mortgage rates and financial-stress indices change: their volatility, their co-movement, or both?

02

Data used

Drive FRED files VIXCLS, T10Y2Y, DGS2, MORTGAGE30US, NFCI and STLFSI4 (retrieved 2026-11-10; Drive ids and sha256 in manifest.json). Each is averaged to Friday weeks; then log VIX and the other levels are differenced and z-scored. That leaves n = 1,709 weeks, 1994-01-14 → 2026-10-09. Only aggregates are shown.

03

What we ran

methods/changeforest/applications/1/run.py runs changeforest(Z, "random_forest", "bs") with default settings. As a pre-processing check, the same call runs on levels. Robustness covers seeds 0–9, drop-one-series, the first 70% of the sample, Binseg-l2/rbf with the same K, k-means, 5 iid shuffles, 5 VAR(1) simulations, and a prewhitened rerun.

07

Limitations

  • Weekly averaging of daily data, and the smoothed NFCI and STLFSI indices, leave strong autocorrelation even after differencing. changeforest assumes independent rows within a segment.
  • The prewhitening VAR(1) is fitted on the full sample, so it can absorb some of the real regime structure. Treat the 12 dates as a conservative set.
  • The prewhitening remedy was added after the null failed. It is a post hoc fix that hasn't yet been tested on fresh data.
08

Takeaway

Differencing alone wasn't enough here. The skill now checks persistence and prewhitens automatically (--prewhiten auto), and it always runs the VAR(1) null. An iid shuffle that returns 0 breaks is not evidence the breaks are real.
