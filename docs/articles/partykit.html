The short version

  • What it is. Trees that split only when a permutation test says a covariate is associated with the response (ctree), or only where a regression's coefficients are unstable (MOB). The output is a few if-then rules you can read.
  • Status: recommended (scoped), awaiting approval. Not promoted until Jared approves it (Stage 4b). All three applications reproduced; the tree structure matched exactly. The county cross-section clears every gate. Both time-series uses fail a circular-shift null (60% and 53% spurious split rates), and the regime tree lost to the historical mean as a forecaster.
  • Novelty: well-known. partykit has been on CRAN for many years and is widely used, so it is less relevant to the scout's goal of finding diamonds in the rough. It is kept as the reference method for the regime-tree skill.
  • What the draft skill says: use it for cross-sectional subgroups and for describing state-dependent slopes. On time series, run the shift null, and never present the tree as a forecaster.

02

The math, plainly

ctree. In a node, for each covariate \(X_j\), form the linear statistic \(T_j=\sum_i g_j(X_{ji})\,h(Y_i)^\top\). Under the null of independence, permuting \(Y\) gives \(T_j\) a known mean \(\mu_j\) and covariance \(\Sigma_j\), so the standardized statistic

$$c_j=\max_k\left|\frac{(T_j-\mu_j)_k}{\sqrt{(\Sigma_j)_{kk}}}\right|$$

has a p-value \(p_j\). Split on the covariate with the smallest Bonferroni-adjusted \(p_j\), and stop when none is below \(\alpha\). Because variable selection is a test rather than a search over cut points, a variable with many possible splits gets no unfair advantage. That bias is what CART suffers from.

MOB. Fit a model \(y=x^\top\beta+\varepsilon\) in the node. For each partitioning variable \(z_j\), order the score contributions \(\psi(y_i,x_i,\hat\beta)\) by \(z_j\) and test their cumulative sums for fluctuation (parameter instability). Split on the most unstable \(z_j\) at the cut point that maximizes the two-segment log-likelihood, then repeat.

03

Why it is unusual, and fast

  • It stops by statistical test instead of being grown large and pruned back, so trees stay small (4–8 leaves here) and come with p-values.
  • MOB answers a different question than a regular tree: does the slope change with the state? In our data it put a financial-conditions variable ahead of calendar time (time p = 0.83).
  • Measured here: ctree fit 30,740 rows in 0.07 s (CART: 0.30 s). The 300 block-bootstrap refits took a few seconds.
