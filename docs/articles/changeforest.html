The short version

  • What it is. A change-point detector that asks a random forest "can you tell rows before time s from rows after it?" and turns the answer into a likelihood ratio. It catches breaks in volatility, correlation or shape, not only the mean.
  • Status: recommended, awaiting approval. Not promoted until Jared approves it (Stage 4b). Novelty: niche. All three applications reproduced exactly in a clean environment. Two cleared every gate; the third cleared them after prewhitening.
  • Lesson the skill now enforces: persistence fools it. On levels it found 63 evenly spaced breaks, and even on weekly changes a break-free VAR(1) simulation produced 20–37. Check persistence and run the VAR(1) null every time.
01

Citation

Londschien, M., Bühlmann, P. & Kovács, S. (2023). "Random Forests for Change Point Detection." Journal of Machine Learning Research 24(216):1–45. Package changeforest v1.2.1: Rust core, Python and R bindings. Baselines in this study: change_in_mean (same package), ruptures 1.1.10 Binseg (l2 and rbf), scikit-learn k-means.

02

The math, plainly

Take a segment of rows \((u, v]\) and a candidate split \(s\). Label rows by side and fit a classifier; let \(\hat\pi(x_i)\) be its out-of-bag probability that row \(i\) belongs to the left part. If the two sides really come from different distributions, the classifier beats the base rate, and the classifier-based log-likelihood ratio

$$G(u,s,v)=\sum_{i=u+1}^{s}\log\frac{\hat\pi(x_i)}{(s-u)/(v-u)}+\sum_{i=s+1}^{v}\log\frac{1-\hat\pi(x_i)}{(v-s)/(v-u)}$$

is large. Binary segmentation then picks the best \(s\), keeps it only if the gain survives a permutation-style test, and recurses on both halves. Swap the forest for a k-nearest-neighbour classifier and you get the knn variant; swap in a Gaussian mean model and you get change_in_mean, our baseline.

03

Why it is unusual, and fast

  • Non-parametric and multivariate. No distribution is assumed, so a break in correlation or variance counts as much as a break in the mean. On all three of our panels the mean-change baseline found 0 breaks while changeforest found structure.
  • One forest scores every split. Out-of-bag predictions from a single fit score a whole grid of candidate splits, and a coarse-then-fine search keeps the number of fits small. The core is Rust.
  • Measured here: 16,000 × 5 synthetic rows in 0.72 s, and the 9,174-day panel in about 1 s. ruptures PELT-rbf took 25.7 s at n = 2,000 in the scout run. Timings depend on the machine.
